The Hardest Pieces in the Library — and Why the List Is Full of Songs

Every piece on this site carries a difficulty level from L1 to L10, and behind that badge is a number from 0 to 100. It is not an opinion. Each of the 2,433 transcriptions was parsed and measured: notes per second, how far the hands travel, how many notes land at the same instant, how wide the pitch range runs, how many accidentals interrupt the pattern.

So here is the top of that ranking — the forty transcriptions in the library that measure hardest.

The problem with this list

Look at it for ten seconds and something is off. Beethoven's Moonlight Sonata finale at the top: fine, that is genuinely one of the hardest things here. Flight of the Bumblebee, Chopin's Op. 28 No. 19, a Gigout toccata, a Rachmaninoff prelude: all reasonable.

But O Mio Babbino Caro? Wolf's Nachtzauber? A Brahms Lied? These are not pieces a pianist would name if you asked for the hardest music on the site. They are art songs, and they are here because of exactly what our measurement does not know.

What the score is actually measuring

The number describes the transcription, not the piece.

When an art song is written into letter notes, the singer's line and the piano accompaniment are folded into a single system of notes. The result, measured mechanically, looks brutal: more notes sounding at once, a wider range from the bass of the accompaniment to the top of the vocal line, and constant chromatic movement, because a singer's melody moves in ways a pianist's right hand often does not.

For an actual pianist playing an actual song, none of that is the real workload — the singer takes the top line, and the piano part alone is frequently very manageable. But in letter notes, where you are playing every sounding note, the transcription really is that dense. So the score is not wrong. It is answering a narrower question than the one you probably asked: not "how hard is this piece" but "how hard is it to play every note in this file".

Why we are showing you the unfiltered list

We tried to filter the songs out. It does not work.

Words like Lied, Gesänge, Mélodies and Songs catch most of them, but the ones that matter slip straight through. O Mio Babbino Caro is an aria with no such word in the title. Debussy's Ariettes oubliées is a song cycle. Josephine Lang's Am Bache, Boulanger's Clairières dans le ciel, Holmès's Les Sept Ivresses — all songs, none catchable by pattern. Any rule tight enough to remove them removes real piano music too.

We could have published a filtered list anyway and hoped nobody checked. Publishing the honest one, with the caveat attached, is more useful: now you know how to read every level badge on the site.

How to read the levels, then

For solo keyboard music, trust the number. Preludes, sonatas, rags, dances, studies — the measurement matches what your hands will feel, and L1 to L10 is a reliable ladder.

For songs and arias, read the level as an upper bound. It tells you how demanding the full transcription is with every note played. If you sing it, or if someone else does, the piano part on its own is usually one or two levels easier than the badge says.

Use the hands-separate control to find out. Press Right or Left in the practice bar and play one side at a time. On a song transcription this immediately reveals how the difficulty is distributed — and often shows that the part you actually wanted to play was never the hard one.

What genuinely is hardest here

If you want the real answer to the question people mean when they ask, it is roughly this: the third movement of the Moonlight Sonata, which measures 100 out of 100 and deserves it — presto agitato, relentless arpeggios, no rest anywhere. Flight of the Bumblebee, for the note rate alone. The fastest of Chopin's Op. 28 preludes. Gottschalk's Sospiro. A handful of late-Romantic salon showpieces by composers most people have never heard of, written to be difficult on purpose, for pianists who wanted to be seen being difficult.

None of them are where you should start. That is what the easiest pieces are for.
