The Easiest Beethoven Pieces for Beginners

Beethoven has a fearsome reputation — thundering symphonies, ferocious sonatas, the stormy finale of the Moonlight. But he also wrote music that a beginner can genuinely play, and one of the most famous melodies in the world happens to be among the easiest pieces in the whole repertoire.

Beethoven (born December 1770 in Bonn; died 26 March 1827 in Vienna) was a German composer whose work drove the transition from the Classical era to the Romantic. (Wikipedia)

Start here

Ode to Joy (easy) The single best Beethoven starting point, and one of the best first pieces on the whole site. It's the melody from the finale of his Symphony No. 9, completed in 1824, setting words from Friedrich Schiller's 1785 poem "An die Freude." The tune was later adopted as the official Anthem of Europe. (Wikipedia)

Why it's so beginner-friendly: the melody moves almost entirely between neighbouring keys — your hand barely has to travel. You can have it sounding right in a single sitting.

Next steps

Sonata Op. 49 No. 2 — Menuetto (easy) Real Beethoven sonata music that's actually approachable. A graceful minuet with clear, repeating phrases — a genuine "I'm playing a Beethoven sonata" moment without the usual difficulty.

Sonata Op. 79 — Andante (easy) A gentle, songlike slow movement. Excellent for practising an even, singing right hand.

His songs — the overlooked easy Beethoven

Beethoven's 8 Lieder, Op. 52 are short, charming and much simpler than his piano works. They're a lovely, less-obvious way to spend time with him:

  • Marmotte (easy) — brief and haunting
  • Das Blümchen Wunderhold (easy)
  • Mollys Abschied (easy)
  • Urians Reise um die Welt (easy)

The famous ones — and when to attempt them

Für Elise (medium) — the piece everyone wants. Its famous opening section is genuinely approachable; the contrasting middle sections are harder. Dated 27 April 1810 on its now-lost manuscript, it wasn't published until 1867, about 40 years after his death. (Wikipedia) (Walkthrough: How to Learn Für Elise)

Moonlight Sonata — 1st movement (medium) — slow, quiet, and built on a repeating pattern, so it's far more achievable than its reputation suggests. Completed in 1801; Beethoven never called it "Moonlight" — the nickname spread after his death. (Wikipedia)) (Walkthrough: How to Learn the Moonlight Sonata)

Leave for much later: the Moonlight's third movement — it's genuinely advanced.

A sensible Beethoven path

  1. Ode to Joy — get a real Beethoven melody under your fingers this week.
  2. Sonata Op. 49 No. 2 Menuetto — your first sonata movement.
  3. Für Elise, opening section only — the famous part.
  4. Moonlight Sonata, 1st movement — slow, controlled, beautiful.
