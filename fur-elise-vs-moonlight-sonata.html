Für Elise vs the Moonlight Sonata: Which Should You Learn First?

These are the two pieces beginners ask for by name, and almost everyone assumes the answer is Für Elise. It is shorter, it is cheerful, and the opening is famous enough to hum. The Moonlight Sonata sounds like the serious one.

We measured both. The assumption is wrong, and the reason is interesting.

The Moonlight scores lower, moves at little more than half the speed, and fits on a keyboard less than half the width. On a 49-key keyboard you can play the entire first movement of the Moonlight Sonata; you cannot play Für Elise at all.

Why Für Elise feels easier than it measures

Because the part everyone knows is easier than the piece. Für Elise is a rondo: the famous falling E–D♯–E returns three times, and between those returns are two episodes that are nothing like it. The second episode has a running left hand and a passage of repeated notes that is the hardest thing in the piece by a wide margin. Most people who "know Für Elise" know the first thirty seconds.

The Moonlight is the reverse. It has no easy part and no hard part. It is one texture — a rising triplet figure under a slow melody — held for six minutes. Learn eight bars and you have learned the method for the whole movement.

The thing nobody warns you about

The Moonlight Sonata is in C♯ minor: four sharps in the key signature, and 23% of the notes in our transcription are black keys, against 13% for Für Elise. If you have been playing for a month, that is a real obstacle — not because black keys are harder to press, but because your hand has not yet learned to sit in a position where they fall naturally under the fingers.

Für Elise is in A minor, which has none. That is its genuine advantage, and it is the only one.

What we would actually tell you

Learn the opening of Für Elise first, because finishing something matters more than anything else at the start, and thirty recognisable seconds is a real achievement.

Then learn the Moonlight properly, all of it, slowly. It will teach you more: an even triplet accompaniment, a melody sustained over a moving inner voice, and the patience to hold one texture without rushing. Those three things transfer to a hundred other pieces. The Für Elise episodes transfer to almost nothing.

And when you go back to finish Für Elise afterwards, the episodes will be much easier than they would have been.

Both, in letter notes

  • Für Elise — A minor, 76-key reach, our score 43
  • Moonlight Sonata, 1st movement — C♯ minor, fits 49 keys, our score 40
