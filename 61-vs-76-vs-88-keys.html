61, 76 or 88 Keys: How Many Do You Actually Need?

The standard advice is 88 weighted keys. It is good advice and it answers a question nobody asked, because the person asking has a budget and a desk.

Here is the question with an exact answer: how much music can you play on each size? We parsed all 2,433 transcriptions in the library and recorded the pitch span of every one — lowest note to highest. If the span fits the keyboard, the piece fits.

The number that matters is the gap, not the total

Going from 61 keys to 76 buys you 18 percentage points — 425 more pieces. Going from 76 to 88 buys you two — 55 pieces, and they are the widest, hardest music in the collection.

That is where the curve flattens. If you are choosing between 76 and 88 keys on price, the honest answer is that the last twelve keys will cost you a great deal of money to unlock 2% of this library, and you will not reach that 2% for years.

If you are choosing between 61 and 76, that is a real decision with a real 425-piece difference behind it.

What 61 keys locks you out of

Not what you would expect. It is not the hard pieces — it is the long ones. Music that goes on for nine minutes tends to visit both ends of the instrument at some point, and one bar near the end is enough to make a piece not fit.

Concretely, from the pieces people ask for most:

  • Fits 49 keys: Moonlight Sonata, 1st movement (46), Gymnopédie No. 1 (49), Canon in D (49), Prelude in C (46)
  • Fits 61 keys: Maple Leaf Rag (61)
  • Needs 76 keys: Für Elise (68), The Entertainer (62), Clair de Lune (71)

Für Elise not fitting on a 61-key keyboard surprises everyone. Its famous opening sits comfortably in the middle; the second episode does not.

Weighted keys matter more than the count

If there is one thing to spend money on, it is not width. A 61-key weighted keyboard will build a hand that transfers to a real piano. An 88-key unweighted one will not, and the habits it teaches — no dynamic control, no finger strength — take months to unlearn.

Width limits which pieces you can play. Weight limits how well you will ever play them.
